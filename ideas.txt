INCOME IDEAS FOR A FOREST SCIENTIST IN COMOX, BC

Iteration 1

1. Post-fire drone hotspot and orthomosaic subcontracting. Private drone firms such as SuavAir and Stinson Aerial Services fly thermal hotspot detection, orthomosaics and LiDAR on BC wildfires under contract to the BC Wildfire Service, and they recruit pilots for the summer surge. A forest scientist who can fly and also turn imagery into burn severity maps is more useful to them than a pilot alone. Approach the firms directly each spring and register on BC Bid for small imaging tenders. Day rates for contract drone pilots on fires typically run 500 to 900 CAD, higher with your own thermal aircraft. Payment usually follows within 30 days of invoice once on a crew. You will need at least a Basic certificate, commercial drone liability insurance of 1 to 2 million CAD, WorkSafeBC coverage as a sole proprietor if the prime requires it, and GST registration once revenue passes 30,000 CAD. Verified: BCWS has used contracted drone pilots for post-fire hotspot searches. Source: https://www.vancouverisawesome.com/highlights/this-squamish-man-spent-the-summer-looking-for-wildfire-hotspots-4358465 Tag: MEDIUM

2. Advanced RPAS upgrade for Comox Valley waterfront and shoreline surveys. Comox sits under the controlled airspace of 19 Wing Comox, so much local drone work legally needs an Advanced certificate and NAV CANADA authorisation. Holding one opens paid shoreline erosion surveys, marina and dock inspections, strata roof and tree hazard imagery, and before and after records for riparian restoration groups. Market through strata managers, marinas in Comox and Courtenay, and local stewardship societies. Realistic rates are 400 to 1,200 CAD per job including processing. The upgrade takes a few weeks: a small advanced exam at 80 percent and an in-person flight review with an approved organisation, typically 300 to 600 CAD. First payment within a month of certification is realistic. Carry drone liability insurance and a municipal business licence from Comox or Courtenay. Verified: Advanced requires the online exam plus an in-person flight review. Source: https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/getting-drone-pilot-certificate Tag: FAST

3. Urban tree canopy assessments for small BC municipalities using open LiDAR. The province publishes free classified point clouds, canopy height models and DTMs through LidarBC. Many small towns and regional districts need canopy cover baselines, change detection and planting priority maps for urban forest strategies but cannot justify a large consultancy. A reproducible R workflow producing canopy maps, heat vulnerability overlays and a short report is a strong fit. Find work through CivicInfo BC, BC Bid and direct approaches to parks managers. A small canopy study pays 8,000 to 25,000 CAD. Procurement cycles mean first payment in two to four months. Professional liability insurance is often requested, and if the work strays into forest management prescriptions it may fall under the Foresters Act, so confine scope to mapping and analysis unless registered with the Forest Professionals BC. Verified: LidarBC distributes free point clouds and canopy structure products. Source: https://lidar.gov.bc.ca/ Tag: SLOW

4. Data package and reproducibility preparation for ecology authors. Journals increasingly require deposited data and code, and repositories like Dryad run curation checks for readability, metadata and reuse. Many authors, especially those without institutional sponsorship, struggle to assemble clean data, a README, and runnable R scripts. Offer a fixed-fee service under your own name that turns a messy analysis folder into a deposit-ready package and a reproducible R Markdown or Quarto document. Find clients through academic networks, ecology Slack and Mastodon communities, and word of mouth from graduate supervisors. Fees of 400 to 1,500 CAD per package are realistic, invoiced on delivery within two to three weeks. No licence is required; charge GST once over the small-supplier threshold, and note that foreign clients are generally zero-rated. Verified: Dryad's curation team checks file readability, metadata and reuse potential, and unsponsored authors pay the charges themselves. Source: https://datadryad.org/help/requirements/costs Tag: FAST

5. Sessional teaching in GIS and environmental science at North Island College. NIC has a campus in Courtenay and hires sessional instructors for credit and continuing education courses, including GIS, environmental and applied science content. A PhD with 20 years of applied remote sensing is well qualified for evening or short-course delivery. Watch the NIC careers page and contact department chairs directly with a course proposal. Instructor positions at NIC average roughly 89,000 CAD a year full time, so a single sessional section prorates to several thousand dollars; continuing education short courses pay less per hour but are easier to start. Payment runs through payroll, so there is no GST, but hiring follows the term calendar, so expect three months or more from first contact. This is employment income, so check that no conflict clause in a current contract prevents it. Verified: NIC instructor salaries average about 89,293 CAD. Most platforms pay monthly, so first payment arrives three to six weeks after the first job. No licence is needed; report as self-employment income, and services to foreign platforms are generally zero-rated for GST. 9. Mapping and drone support subcontracting to Qualified Environmental Professionals doing riparian assessments. Under BC's Riparian Areas Protection Regulation, development near fish streams needs an assessment signed by a registered QEP, including stream width, reach breaks and setback mapping. You do not need to sign reports to supply the parts QEP firms find tedious: drone orthomosaics of the site, SPEA polygons in QGIS, canopy and vegetation mapping and report figures. Vancouver Island firms such as Current Environmental and Hill Environmental do this work steadily. Pitch a per-site package at 600 to 1,500 CAD. With a small firm, first payment inside a month is realistic because turnaround on residential lots is quick. Carry drone liability and professional liability insurance, and do not present opinions that only a registered QEP may give. Verified: RAPR requires the QEP to be registered under the Professional Governance Act and the assessment to cover stream and setback determinations. Source: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/plants-animals-ecosystems/fish/aquatic-habitat-management/riparian-areas-regulation Tag: FAST

Paid work can start within a season once certified. Verified: Level 2 Instructor requires 15 logged days of Level 1 instruction and two lead Level 1 courses. Source: https://paddlecanada.com/levels/sea-kayak/sea-kayak-level-2-instructor/ Tag: SLOW

11. Expert evaluator for Horizon Europe and other EU research calls. The European Commission pays outside experts to score proposals, and forestry, biodiversity, land use and Earth observation calls need reviewers with exactly this profile. Registration on the Funding and Tenders Portal expert database is open to any nationality, and most evaluation is now done remotely. Being picked can take months after registering, but once contracted, payment follows within about a month of completing the task. Report it as self-employment income; the services are exported, so GST does not apply to the EU fee. Check the declared conflicts of interest so that no call involves carbon market work. Verified: experts normally receive 450 EUR per full day, and registration is open to all nationalities. Source: https://cinea.ec.europa.eu/about-us/key-documents/experts_en Tag: SLOW

12. Expert witness and litigation support in tree, timber and land disputes. Neighbour tree cutting, timber trespass on private woodlots, landslide and windthrow claims, and insurance disputes over forest damage all need an independent scientist who can reconstruct what stood on a site from historical imagery, LiDAR and stumps. Remote sensing change detection is a rare and valuable skill in this setting. Work comes from BC litigation lawyers, insurers and expert referral directories such as JurisPro and SEAK. Forestry experts typically bill 200 to 450 CAD an hour. The first retainer can take a few months, but many firms pay an upfront retainer. Carry professional liability insurance, keep your CV and publications current, and be careful: timber valuation and prescriptive advice on BC land may count as the practice of professional forestry, so stay within scientific analysis unless registered. Verified: forestry expert witnesses bill roughly 200 to 450 an hour. Source: https://www.law.com/expert-witness/directory/forestry/ Tag: SLOW
