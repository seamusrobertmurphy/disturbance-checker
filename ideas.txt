INCOME IDEAS FOR A FOREST SCIENTIST IN COMOX, BC

Iteration 1

1. Post-fire drone hotspot and orthomosaic subcontracting. Private drone firms such as SuavAir and Stinson Aerial Services fly thermal hotspot detection, orthomosaics and LiDAR on BC wildfires under contract to the BC Wildfire Service, and they recruit pilots for the summer surge. A forest scientist who can fly and also turn imagery into burn severity maps is more useful to them than a pilot alone. Approach the firms directly each spring and register on BC Bid for small imaging tenders. Day rates for contract drone pilots on fires typically run 500 to 900 CAD, higher with your own thermal aircraft. Payment usually follows within 30 days of invoice once on a crew. You will need at least a Basic certificate, commercial drone liability insurance of 1 to 2 million CAD, WorkSafeBC coverage as a sole proprietor if the prime requires it, and GST registration once revenue passes 30,000 CAD. Verified: BCWS has used contracted drone pilots for post-fire hotspot searches. Source: https://www.vancouverisawesome.com/highlights/this-squamish-man-spent-the-summer-looking-for-wildfire-hotspots-4358465 Tag: MEDIUM

2. Advanced RPAS upgrade for Comox Valley waterfront and shoreline surveys. Comox sits under the controlled airspace of 19 Wing Comox, so much local drone work legally needs an Advanced certificate and NAV CANADA authorisation. Holding one opens paid shoreline erosion surveys, marina and dock inspections, strata roof and tree hazard imagery, and before and after records for riparian restoration groups. Market through strata managers, marinas in Comox and Courtenay, and local stewardship societies. Realistic rates are 400 to 1,200 CAD per job including processing. The upgrade takes a few weeks: a small advanced exam at 80 percent and an in-person flight review with an approved organisation, typically 300 to 600 CAD. First payment within a month of certification is realistic. Carry drone liability insurance and a municipal business licence from Comox or Courtenay. Verified: Advanced requires the online exam plus an in-person flight review. Source: https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/getting-drone-pilot-certificate Tag: FAST

3. Urban tree canopy assessments for small BC municipalities using open LiDAR. The province publishes free classified point clouds, canopy height models and DTMs through LidarBC. Many small towns and regional districts need canopy cover baselines, change detection and planting priority maps for urban forest strategies but cannot justify a large consultancy. A reproducible R workflow producing canopy maps, heat vulnerability overlays and a short report is a strong fit. Find work through CivicInfo BC, BC Bid and direct approaches to parks managers. A small canopy study pays 8,000 to 25,000 CAD. Procurement cycles mean first payment in two to four months. Professional liability insurance is often requested, and if the work strays into forest management prescriptions it may fall under the Foresters Act, so confine scope to mapping and analysis unless registered with the Forest Professionals BC. Verified: LidarBC distributes free point clouds and canopy structure products. Source: https://lidar.gov.bc.ca/ Tag: SLOW

4. Data package and reproducibility preparation for ecology authors. Journals increasingly require deposited data and code, and repositories like Dryad run curation checks for readability, metadata and reuse. Many authors, especially those without institutional sponsorship, struggle to assemble clean data, a README, and runnable R scripts. Offer a fixed-fee service under your own name that turns a messy analysis folder into a deposit-ready package and a reproducible R Markdown or Quarto document. Find clients through academic networks, ecology Slack and Mastodon communities, and word of mouth from graduate supervisors. Fees of 400 to 1,500 CAD per package are realistic, invoiced on delivery within two to three weeks. No licence is required; charge GST once over the small-supplier threshold, and note that foreign clients are generally zero-rated. Verified: Dryad's curation team checks file readability, metadata and reuse potential, and unsponsored authors pay the charges themselves. Source: https://datadryad.org/help/requirements/costs Tag: FAST

5. Sessional teaching in GIS and environmental science at North Island College. NIC has a campus in Courtenay and hires sessional instructors for credit and continuing education courses, including GIS, environmental and applied science content. A PhD with 20 years of applied remote sensing is well qualified for evening or short-course delivery. Watch the NIC careers page and contact department chairs directly with a course proposal. Instructor positions at NIC average roughly 89,000 CAD a year full time, so a single sessional section prorates to several thousand dollars; continuing education short courses pay less per hour but are easier to start. Payment runs through payroll, so there is no GST, but hiring follows the term calendar, so expect three months or more from first contact. This is employment income, so check that no conflict clause in a current contract prevents it. Verified: NIC instructor salaries average about 89,293 CAD. Source: https://www.glassdoor.ca/Salary/North-Island-College-Salaries-E479340.htm Tag: SLOW

Iteration 2

6. Freelance scientific editing for non-native English authors. Editing services such as AJE, Editage and Kolabtree pay subject specialists to polish manuscripts in forestry, ecology and environmental science before journal submission. Twenty years of writing and publishing makes a PhD in this field a strong applicant, and the work is done openly under your own name from home. Apply through each service's editor recruitment page and pass an editing test. AJE pays per word on a first come basis, and typical freelance scientific editing works out at 21 to 40 USD an hour, rising with speed and specialist projects; Kolabtree lets you set your own rate from about 30 USD an hour. Most platforms pay monthly, so first payment arrives three to six weeks after the first job. No licence is needed; report as self-employment income, and services to foreign platforms are generally zero-rated for GST. Verified: freelance scientific editors earn about 21 to 40 USD an hour, and AJE pays per word. Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Freelance-Scientific-Editor Tag: MEDIUM

7. Premium spatial statistics and remote sensing contracts on Upwork. Upwork carries a steady flow of GIS, Google Earth Engine, land cover classification, LiDAR and R spatial modelling jobs from universities, NGOs and agtech firms, paid in USD. The median GIS rate there is only about 25 USD an hour, so the play is not to compete on price but to position as a PhD specialist in spatial statistics and forest remote sensing, bidding only on complex jobs at 75 to 120 USD an hour and filtering out any carbon market clients. Expect a slow first month while building reviews, then first payment two to three weeks after a milestone is approved. Upwork takes a 10 percent fee. No licence is required; keep records for self-employment tax and GST once over the threshold, with most foreign clients zero-rated. Verified: Upwork's median GIS developer rate is 25 USD with most between 20 and 30 USD. Source: https://www.upwork.com/hire/gis-developers/cost/ Tag: MEDIUM

8. GIS, data management and monitoring design support for First Nations Guardian programs. Guardian programs across coastal BC run ecological monitoring on their territories and often need help building field data systems, monitoring protocols, R dashboards and maps, plus training for Guardians. Federal Guardian funding was renewed in 2026 at about 231 million CAD over five years, and several programs budget for technical contractors. Work comes through relationships with Nations and umbrella bodies such as Coastal First Nations and the Nanwakolas Council, so expect a slow start and a strong emphasis on data sovereignty and OCAP principles. Contracts of 5,000 to 40,000 CAD are typical, at 80 to 120 CAD an hour. Payment follows funding cycles, often three months or more. Carry professional liability insurance and register for GST when over the threshold; many band governments are GST exempt on purchases, so learn the exemption paperwork. Screen out any Guardian work tied to carbon credit projects. Verified: Canada committed 231 million CAD over five years to Indigenous Guardians programs. Source: https://thenarwhal.ca/indigenous-guardian-investment/ Tag: SLOW

9. Mapping and drone support subcontracting to Qualified Environmental Professionals doing riparian assessments. Under BC's Riparian Areas Protection Regulation, development near fish streams needs an assessment signed by a registered QEP, including stream width, reach breaks and setback mapping. You do not need to sign reports to supply the parts QEP firms find tedious: drone orthomosaics of the site, SPEA polygons in QGIS, canopy and vegetation mapping and report figures. Vancouver Island firms such as Current Environmental and Hill Environmental do this work steadily. Pitch a per-site package at 600 to 1,500 CAD. With a small firm, first payment inside a month is realistic because turnaround on residential lots is quick. Carry drone liability and professional liability insurance, and do not present opinions that only a registered QEP may give. Verified: RAPR requires the QEP to be registered under the Professional Governance Act and the assessment to cover stream and setback determinations. Source: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/plants-animals-ecosystems/fish/aquatic-habitat-management/riparian-areas-regulation Tag: FAST

10. Sea kayak instruction and natural history guiding. His paddling experience plus deep knowledge of coastal forests, estuaries and shoreline ecology suits teaching and interpretive guiding around Baynes Sound, Denman and Hornby Islands and the Broughton. The path is Paddle Canada certification: Level 1 Instructor first, then Level 2 Instructor after 15 logged teaching days and leading two Level 1 courses. Find work with Comox Valley and Quadra Island outfitters, or run your own weekend skills courses. Contract instructors and guides earn about 180 to 300 CAD a day, more for multi-day trips. Paid work can start within a season once certified. You will need a current wilderness first aid certificate, liability insurance through Paddle Canada or the outfitter, and for commercial trips in parks a BC Parks permit, which outfitters normally hold. Verified: Level 2 Instructor requires 15 logged days of Level 1 instruction and two lead Level 1 courses. Source: https://paddlecanada.com/levels/sea-kayak/sea-kayak-level-2-instructor/ Tag: SLOW
